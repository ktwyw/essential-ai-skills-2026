# Module 5: Domain Expertise

> AI multiplies what you know. It does not replace it.

## Learning goals

By the end of this module you will be able to:

- Explain why expertise makes AI more valuable, not less.
- Recognize the risk of skill erosion from over-reliance on AI.
- Use AI as a tutor to deepen your own understanding.

## Key ideas

### Expertise drives every other skill

Deep knowledge of your field lets you ask the right questions (module 1), spot plausible but wrong answers (module 2), and decide what to delegate (module 4). A doctor, lawyer, or engineer using AI gets far more value than a novice using the same tool, because they know what good looks like.

### The erosion risk

If AI does all the hard thinking, your own skills can fade, or never develop. This risk is greatest for students and people early in their careers, who have not yet built the expertise that makes AI safe to rely on.

### Use AI as a tutor, not only an assistant

| Assistant mode | Tutor mode |
|----------------|------------|
| "Write my essay." | "Critique my essay's argument." |
| "Solve this problem." | "Give me a hint, not the answer." |
| "Explain this concept." | "Quiz me on this concept." |
| "Fix my code." | "Explain why my code fails." |

Both modes are useful. The key is to choose deliberately, and to use tutor mode whenever the goal is learning.

## Practice

1. **Expert vs novice.** Ask an AI a question in your field of study. Then ask a friend from a different field to evaluate the answer. What did you notice that they missed?
2. **Tutor mode.** Pick a topic you are studying. Ask the AI to quiz you with five questions, one at a time, and to explain your mistakes.
3. **Hints only.** Work through a problem set asking the AI only for hints. Compare how much you remember a week later with a problem you solved by asking for the full answer.
4. **Find the error.** Ask an AI to explain a concept you know very well. Find something it got wrong, oversimplified, or left out.

## Common mistakes

- **Using assistant mode when the goal is learning.** The answer appears; the skill does not.
- **Trusting an explanation you cannot test.** If you do not yet know the field, you cannot tell a subtle error from a fact - which is the argument for building the field first.
- **Never closing the chat.** The test of learning is doing it again alone (module 8).
- **Letting the AI set the standard.** "Good enough for the AI" is not a standard; your field's is.

## Reflection

- In which areas are you expert enough to judge AI output? In which are you not?
- Which tasks will you keep doing yourself, so your skills keep growing?
- How should students use AI in courses where the goal is learning, not output?

## Key takeaway

Your expertise is what turns AI from a source of plausible text into a genuinely powerful tool. Keep building it.
