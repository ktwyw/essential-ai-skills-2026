# Module 4: Decomposition and Delegation

> Working with AI agents resembles managing a capable new colleague more than operating software.

## Learning goals

By the end of this module you will be able to:

- Break a large goal into well-defined tasks.
- Write a delegation brief that gives an AI agent what it needs to succeed.
- Decide which tasks to delegate and which to keep.

## Key ideas

### From answers to actions

AI is moving from answering single questions to carrying out multi-step work: researching a topic, editing files, running code, and reporting back. These systems are often called **agents**. The more an AI can do on its own, the more your role shifts to planning and supervising.

### Good tasks have three parts

Every piece of delegated work needs:

1. **A clear outcome:** what "done" looks like.
2. **Enough context:** background, files, constraints, examples.
3. **A way to check:** how you will know it was done correctly.

Vague delegation produces vague work, from people and machines alike.

### Decide what not to delegate

| Delegate | Keep for yourself |
|----------|-------------------|
| High-volume, repetitive work | Final decisions and accountability |
| First drafts | Sensitive judgment calls |
| Research and summarizing | Knowledge only you have |
| Formatting and cleanup | Relationships and trust |
| Routine code | Choosing the goal itself |

### Check in at milestones

For longer tasks, ask the AI to stop and show its plan before it starts, and to report after each major step. It is much cheaper to correct direction early than to redo finished work.

## Practice

1. **Break it down.** Take a big goal, such as "organize a class trip" or "write a research report". Split it into 5 to 10 tasks. For each, mark: AI, me, or both.
2. **Write a brief.** Choose one task from exercise 1. Write a delegation brief with outcome, context, and a check. Give it to an AI and evaluate the result against your check.
3. **Plan first.** Ask an AI to make a plan for a multi-step task *before* doing anything. Review and edit the plan, then let it continue. Did reviewing the plan improve the result?
4. **The boundary.** List three tasks in your studies or work that you would never delegate to AI. Explain why.

## Reflection

- Where did your delegation brief fall short, and what did you add?
- How did checking the plan first change the outcome?
- What does good delegation to AI have in common with good teamwork?

## Key takeaway

The best results come from a deliberate division of labor: AI handles volume and speed, and you handle direction and final decisions.
