# Capstone project ideas

Thirty ideas by field, each sized for four weeks of part-time work. Every one needs the four things the brief asks for: a real problem, a working result, a commit history, and a PROCESS.md that shows prompts, errors caught, division of labour and privacy decisions.

## Engineering and science

1. **Unit-aware calculator** for a course's standard calculations (pipe pressure drop, heat-exchanger sizing, beam deflection), with tests against textbook examples.
2. **Lab-data pipeline:** CSV in, cleaned data, figures and a Markdown report out, for one of your lab courses; verified against one hand calculation per figure.
3. **Formula sheet with checks:** every formula in a course with units, limiting cases and one worked example, generated with AI and verified line by line.
4. **Reproduce a published figure** from a paper's data (many papers share data): document every difference you find.
5. **Error-propagation tool** that takes a formula and measured inputs with uncertainties and reports the result's uncertainty by Monte Carlo.
6. **Safety-data-sheet summariser** for the chemicals in your lab, with every hazard statement checked against the original sheets.
7. **Equipment logbook analysis:** failures and maintenance from a lab or workshop log; which equipment fails most, and when.
8. **Experiment planner:** given factors and ranges, produce a factorial or Latin-square design and the analysis script to go with it.

## Data and computing

9. **Citation checker web page or script** that takes a reference list and reports which DOIs resolve (extend `check_citations.py` to search by title).
10. **Course timetable optimiser** for a small department, with constraints in a readable file.
11. **Energy-use dashboard** for a building or home from meter readings, with a model of what drives consumption.
12. **A small REST API** that serves a dataset you cleaned, with documentation generated and then verified.
13. **Automated regression tests** for an existing script a lab uses, written with AI and checked against known outputs.
14. **A SQL database** of local resources (clinics, repair shops, tutors) with ten queries that answer real questions.

## Study and teaching

15. **Verified quiz bank** for one course: 100 questions generated from notes, every answer checked against the source, with a difficulty tag.
16. **Bilingual technical glossary** (for example English-Kazakh-Russian) for one course, each entry checked against a textbook in each language.
17. **Worked-solution set** for past exam questions, produced in tutor mode, with the AI's errors recorded in a PROCESS log.
18. **Study guide** for a course in Markdown, with a concept map and self-check quizzes.
19. **A teaching demonstration** of prompt injection or hallucination, with a lesson plan and a safe exercise.
20. **A course-policy explainer:** your institution's AI policy rewritten for students as a FAQ, checked against the original.

## Community and professional

21. **A fact-checked guide** to a local process (registering a business, applying for a permit, accessing a service), with every step verified against the official source.
22. **Grant or scholarship finder:** a curated, verified list for your field and country, with deadlines in a machine-readable file.
23. **Meeting-minutes assistant:** a script that turns recordings or notes into structured minutes, with a privacy decision documented.
24. **Accessibility audit** of a department web page, with AI-assisted analysis and manual confirmation of each finding.
25. **A newsletter pipeline** for a student society: collects, summarises and formats, with every summary checked against the source.

## Non-technical options

26. **A researched, fact-checked essay** on a topic in your field, with a complete record of AI use and a verification log of every claim.
27. **A comparison of AI tools** on ten tasks from your discipline, with the outputs, errors found, and a recommendation.
28. **A reading guide** to five papers, each explained with AI help and checked against the paper.
29. **A translation** of one module of this course, with back-translation checks documented.
30. **A "how I learned X with AI" case study:** a skill you built in four weeks, with the prompts that helped, the ones that hurt, and what you would tell a classmate.

## Choosing

Pick something you would want even if AI did not exist. The project is graded on the process - prompts, verification, delegation, responsibility - and a problem you care about makes that process honest.
